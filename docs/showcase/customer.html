The Candidate Line

Candidates book their first interview by phone, any hour, and never hear a word about how they're doing.

A working prototype on synthetic data, built 1 to 5 November 2026.

Runs on AWS for speech, Claude by Anthropic for the conversation, and Twilio for the phone number.

What It Does

  • Checks who is calling by application code and PIN.
  • Books an open first-round slot once the caller confirms it out loud.
  • Answers process questions from your FAQ and names the section it used.
  • Takes a callback request for anything else.
  • Hands a request to move a slot to your coordinator.

What It Never Does

  • No score, ranking, opinion or prediction about any candidate, ever.
  • Nothing from outside your FAQ. When the FAQ has no answer, it says so and offers a callback.
  • Never pretends to be a person. It is designed to say it is automated in its first sentence.
  • Every move and every judgment stays with your people.

Designed with the hiring-law pages on automated tools in view (New York City's Local Law 144 and two Illinois acts). It makes no hiring decision.

What ACME Provides

  • The FAQ, the interview calendar, a phone number and a Slack channel; for production, an AWS account and a database.
  • A coordinator who takes the hand-offs.
  • Sign-off on the list of what the line never does.

It Also Calls Back

  • Calls back an applicant who applied, asks the role's posted questions, then books the interview or has a recruiter call. A person makes every hiring decision.

What You See

  • A page for your recruiters with every call's record: what was heard, what was said, which FAQ section, who handled it, and what it cost.
  • Hand-offs are posted to your team's channel (Slack) the moment the line decides it needs a person, with a link to the call.
  • A reply box on each hand-off, so your coordinator's answer is logged against the call.
  • An off switch. Flip it and every live call ends politely within one exchange.
  • Callers' numbers are masked before anything is stored. No audio is kept.

How We'd Roll It Out

  1. Proof of concept. Synthetic candidates, slots and FAQ. Your team calls it and tries to break it.
  2. Pilot. One role family on your real FAQ and calendar, a person on every hand-off, measured against your past invite-to-booked hours.
  3. Production. Runs in your AWS account, on your calendar and your FAQ, with the call record in your database.
