# ACME candidate FAQ

This is the FAQ the candidate line answers from. Every answer Gus gives from it names the section id he used (for example F3.2). If a question is not covered here, Gus says so and offers a callback rather than guessing.

## F1. The process

### F1.1 What are the stages?
Four stages: an application review, a first-round interview, a take-home exercise, and a final round. Not every role uses every stage; the invitation you received says which stage is next for you.

### F1.2 How long does the whole process take?
Most candidates move from the first-round invitation to a final decision within three to four weeks. The coordinator keeps you informed at each stage. Timelines can stretch around holidays and when interviewers travel.

### F1.3 Who is my contact?
The recruiting coordinator for your role. This line can take a callback request at any time (F8), and the coordinator returns calls within one business day.

## F2. The first-round interview

### F2.1 How long is it, and in what format?
Thirty minutes, by video, with one ACME engineer. There is no coding exercise in the first round; it is a conversation about your experience and about the role.

### F2.2 What should I prepare?
Have a recent project in mind that you can describe in a few minutes: what it did, what you built, and what you would change. Have a question or two about the team. No slides and no code are needed.

### F2.3 Will I be recorded?
No. First-round interviews are not recorded. The interviewer takes brief notes for the hiring team.

### F2.4 Can I ask for a different interviewer?
You can tell the coordinator about a conflict of interest, such as a former manager or a close colleague, and the coordinator will arrange a different interviewer. Preferences about an interviewer's background cannot be accommodated.

## F3. Booking, moving and cancelling

### F3.1 How do I book a slot?
Give the line your application code (ACME followed by four digits) and your four-digit PIN, both printed in your invitation. The line reads out the open slots for the next two weeks and books the one you choose. You receive a confirmation by email within a few minutes.

### F3.2 Can I move my slot?
Once, through the coordinator. Tell the line which slot you want instead and it hands the request to the coordinator during the call; the line does not move a booked slot itself. A second move is the coordinator's decision too.

### F3.3 What if I need to cancel?
Tell the line or the coordinator as early as you can. A cancelled slot can be rebooked for up to two weeks after the cancellation.

### F3.4 What time zone are the slots in?
All slots are in Central time. If you are elsewhere, say your time zone and the line will repeat the slot in it.

### F3.5 What if none of the slots work?
Ask for a callback (F8). The coordinator can open a slot outside the standard times for candidates in other time zones or with caring responsibilities.

## F4. The take-home exercise

### F4.1 How long should it take?
About three hours. It is designed to be done in one sitting, and there is no benefit to spending longer.

### F4.2 When is it due?
Seven days after you receive it. The due time is 17:00 Central on the seventh day.

### F4.3 Can I have an extension?
Yes. Tell the coordinator before the due date; an extension of up to seven more days is given without a reason.

### F4.4 What language or tools may I use?
Any you like. The exercise is reviewed for how it is built and explained, and your README is part of it.

## F5. Logistics and accommodations

### F5.1 How do I join the video interview?
The confirmation email carries a link. Join from a laptop if you can; a phone works too. If the link fails, the interviewer calls the number on your application.

### F5.2 What accommodations are available?
Extra time, a captioned call, an interviewer who can sign, a text-based interview, breaks, or an interview split over two sessions, among others. Ask the coordinator or request a callback; you do not need to give a reason or a diagnosis.

### F5.3 Do I need to travel?
No. Every stage is remote, including the final round.

### F5.4 What if I am running late?
Call this line or email the coordinator. The interviewer waits ten minutes; after that the slot is rebooked at no cost to you.

## F6. After the interview

### F6.1 When will I hear back?
Within five business days of the first-round interview, by email from the coordinator. If you have heard nothing after five business days, request a callback and the coordinator will check.

### F6.2 Can I get feedback?
The coordinator shares a short written summary on request after a final decision. This line does not have access to interview notes or decisions and cannot comment on how an interview went.

### F6.3 Can I reapply?
Yes, after six months, to any role.

## F7. Offers and background checks

### F7.1 What happens at the offer stage?
A recruiter calls, then sends the offer in writing. You have five business days to decide, and more on request.

### F7.2 Is there a background check?
Yes, after an offer is accepted: employment history and, for some roles, a right-to-work check. A third party runs it with your consent, and you see any report before ACME does.

### F7.3 Does ACME sponsor visas?
For some roles. The job description says whether the role is open to sponsorship; if it does not say, ask the coordinator.

## F8. Callbacks and this line

### F8.1 What can this line do?
Answer questions from this FAQ, book a first-round slot, hand a move to the coordinator, and take a callback request. It cannot see interview notes, decisions or scores, and it does not evaluate candidates.

### F8.2 How do I request a callback?
Say that you want a callback and, if you like, the best time to reach you. The coordinator returns calls within one business day, using the number you called from unless you give another.

### F8.3 Is this call recorded?
The call is transcribed to text so the coordinator can act on your request; audio is not kept. Say "stop" at any time to end the call.

### F8.4 Who do I contact about this line?
The coordinator, by the same callback route. Complaints about the line itself are read by a person.

## F9. Screening questions and calls from this line

### F9.1 Why did this line call me?
You applied for an hourly role at ACME and agreed, on the application form, to an automated call about it. The line calls within a few minutes of the application, between 9 AM and 8 PM, Monday to Saturday, in your time zone. It says first that it is automated, and it asks for the four-digit PIN you chose when you applied before it says anything else about the application.

### F9.2 What does the line decide?
Only one thing: whether it offers you an interview time now or a recruiter calls you back. The line asks the questions the employer posted for the role, in the posted words, and writes down each answer as you give it. When every answer fits what the posting asks for, it offers the recruiter's open interview times. When one does not, it says what the posting asks for and that a recruiter will call you within one business day. A person reads your answers and makes every decision about your application; the line makes none.

### F9.3 How do I stop the calls?
Say "stop" or "don't call me" on any call from this line, and it will not call that number again. You can also call this line and say the same. Stopping the calls does not withdraw your application.

### F9.4 What is kept, and for how long?
Your answers are kept as text, in your own words and as the line noted them, with the time of each call and a record of the consent you gave; no audio is kept. Your phone number is kept apart from everything else, is used only to place these calls, and is deleted 30 days after you apply, or at once when you ask the line to stop calling. To have your answers deleted, ask for a callback (F8.2) and say so.
