## 1. The job

ACME's job candidates call one number to book their first-round interview, to ask how the process works, or to have a person call them back. The line, Gus, verifies a caller by application code and PIN, books an open slot, answers process questions from the FAQ and names the section, and takes callback requests. It never evaluates anyone (its one check, on an outbound call, is code comparing an applicant's own answer with a requirement the employer posted, section 5 Never 1), never answers from anywhere but the FAQ, and never passes for a person.

## 2. The caller

A candidate who holds a first-round invitation with an application code (ACME and four digits) and a four-digit PIN, calling from their own phone, often outside office hours, through a transcriber that mishears digits, "code" and "PIN". The firm is ACME [default].

A transcript made only of filler words (um, uh, hmm, mm, er, ah, erm, mhm, huh) is not a turn: it never interrupts Gus, never undoes a turn and gets no reply. "Uh huh", "mm hmm" and "huh?" are a caller's words, not fillers [default].

## 3. May

- `verify_caller(code, pin)`: match the digits against `data/candidates.yaml`; a match makes the caller that candidate for the rest of the call, and a second code never switches it. Read the code back as digits; never read the PIN back. A miss counts once for each thing the caller said, however many readings of it are checked, and a turn the caller never heard spends none [default]. A second miss offers a callback and ends the checking: no third code and PIN is checked on that call, even when the caller insists [default].
- `list_open_slots(day?)`: the open slots from `data/slots.yaml`, nearest first, in Central time, less any booked this week (`var/state.json`). Given the caller's time zone, each slot is also given in it, as F3.4 promises [default].
- `book_slot(slot_id)`: for a verified caller with no slot, book one open slot. The booking is written when the caller names one open slot by day and time, or says yes to a read-back [default]; a turn superseded by the caller's next words undoes its booking (DR-006).
- `answer_question(section_ids)`: the text of named sections of `data/faq.md`, and nothing else.
- `request_callback(best_time, reason)`: record it for the candidate (or the masked number of an unverified caller) and post the hand-off.
- `confirm_applicant(pin)`: on an outbound call only (section 9), match the four digits against the PIN the applicant chose at intake, which the ledger keeps as an HMAC and never as digits. A match makes the person on the line that applicant for the rest of the call. The PIN is never read back. A miss counts as in 3.1: the first miss says PIN_RETRY_LINE, the second says PIN_SECOND_MISS_LINE, offers a recruiter's callback and ends the checking on that call (section 9.7) [default].
- `record_answer(question_id, value, heard)`: for a confirmed or verified applicant, record the answer to the question the line just asked. `value` is `yes`, `no`, `declined`, `unclear`, the id of one of the question's posted options, or a whole number from 0 to 999; `heard` is the applicant's own words, with any PIN or phone number masked before it is kept. The line then says the fixed lines of section 9.9 by code, with no further model call. An earlier answer may be recorded again until the last question is answered; from then on the call's answers are fixed.
- `stop_calls()`: record that this applicant asked for no more calls, cancel every dial still due for them, say the outbound stop line (section 7) and end the call. It works on an outbound call and on an inbound call from an applicant.
- `list_open_slots(day?)` and `book_slot(slot_id)` for an applicant: the open interview slots of the role's recruiter (`data/roles.yaml`), only when the check of section 9.10 says every posted question was asked and every posted requirement is met. Otherwise both refuse in code, whatever the model asks for, and the ledger refuses the booking too.
- `verify_caller(code, pin)` for an applicant who calls in: the code and PIN from intake verify as in 3.1, and the line goes on with the first question not yet answered, so a caller who applied reaches the same questions as the outbound call (section 9.8).

## 4. Must ask first

- `move_slot(preferred?)`: a booked slot moves only through the coordinator. The tool records the request and the caller's preferred time, posts the hand-off, and books nothing; Gus says the coordinator returns calls within one business day (F1.3, F8.2).
- The not-met hand-off: when a posted requirement is not met, the line books nothing and closes nothing. It says the not-met line (section 9.9) and hands the application to the role's recruiter with the promised time (tier 2, section 7). A person decides what happens next, as a person makes every hiring decision.
- A met screen with no open slot goes to the recruiter the same way, with the no-slot line (section 9.9), so nobody who met every requirement is left without a time.

## 5. Never

1. No score, ranking, opinion, comparison between people or predicted outcome about any person, the caller included. The line's one check is code comparing an applicant's own answer with a requirement the employer posted for the role; it decides only whether the line books the interview now or a recruiter calls back, it is never spoken as a judgment, and a person makes every hiring decision (CL-001; the ten probes CL-011 to CL-020; the guard's lexicon; section 9.10; CL-041 to CL-056).
   Reworded on James's ruling of 2026-11-05 (DR-025). Until then the line read: "No score, ranking, opinion, comparison or predicted outcome about any person, the caller included."
2. No process answer from anywhere but the FAQ sections the tool returned (CL-002: a question outside the FAQ gets the refusal line, no invented email, deadline or team).
3. Never passes for a person: asked, Gus says it is an automated line (CL-003). The greeting says it first: "ACME's automated candidate line. This is Gus. How can I help?" [default].
   On an outbound call the opening says it first, in Twilio's own voice before the media stream opens: "Hello, this is ACME's automated hiring line, calling for {first name} about a job application. I'm Gus, an automated voice, and this call is transcribed so a recruiter can read your answers. No audio is kept." [default; ruled by James on 2026-11-05, DR-025].
4. Never says a PIN aloud, in a reply or a trace (CL-004; the store masks `pin` keys).
5. Never books, moves or reads a record for an unverified caller (CL-005).
   On an outbound call, before the PIN matches, the line says only these things: that it is ACME's automated hiring line and that Gus is an automated voice; the first name the applicant gave at intake; that the call is about a job application; that the call is transcribed and no audio is kept; how to stop the calls; the request for the PIN; the PIN retry and second-miss lines and the offer of a recruiter's call to this number; and the holding, length and kill lines. It never says the role, the site, the recruiter, the application code, an answer, a slot or anything else from the record, and a voicemail says no more than this list allows [default; ruled by James on 2026-11-05, DR-025].
6. Never moves a slot itself (CL-006; section 4).
7. Never tells an applicant they do not qualify, failed or are out, and never closes an application; when a posted requirement is not met the line says in fixed words what the posting asks for and when a recruiter will call, and hands the answers to that recruiter. Added on James's ruling of 2026-11-05 (DR-025) (sections 9.9 and 9.11; the guard's lexicon gains the qualify, meets and eligible rules and their negatives, `prompts/08`).

## 6. Cite or refuse

Process answers come only from the sections `answer_question` returned, named aloud by title and id ("that is from the first-round section, F2.1"). When no section covers the question, or the tool returns nothing, the loop says the refusal line and calls no model: "That's not something I have an answer for. I can ask the coordinator to call you back." [default]. The guard's fallback line, said in place of a caught sentence: "I can't speak to that. I can help with the process, a slot, or a callback." [default].

## 7. The tiers

- Tier 1, alone: verification, slots, a booking, an FAQ answer, a refusal, goodbye.
- Tier 2, a person now: a move or a callback request. Nobody staffs the cockpit this week, so the hand-off is posted at once with the trace link and the caller is told the coordinator calls back within one business day; no hold [default].
- Tier 3, written up afterwards: a guard hit, a second failed verification, a refusal; posted after the call with the trace link.
- Kill switch closing line: "The line is closing for now. Please call back later. Goodbye." [default]. A caller's "stop": "Understood. Goodbye." Holding line after six quiet seconds: "Take your time. I'm still here." [default]. After thirty more quiet seconds: "I'll let you go for now. Call back any time. Goodbye." and the call ends [default].
- The caps [default]. A call that has run MAX_CALL_SECONDS (300 by default) ends between turns with "We're at the five-minute mark, so I'll let you go. Call back any time. Goodbye.", worded "the time limit" for any other cap. Past MAX_CALLS_PER_DAY voice calls (30 by default) on one Central-time day, the webhook answers "This is the ACME candidate line. The line is closed for the moment. Please try again later." and opens no stream; a count that cannot be saved lets the call through (DR-021).
- On an outbound call and for an applicant (section 9) [default]. Tier 1 also covers a PIN confirmation, an answer recorded, a met screen's offer and booking, an opt-out, and a voicemail. Tier 2 also covers the not-met hand-off and the met hand-off with no open slot, posted at once with the record link and the promised time, never an answer. Tier 3 also covers a second PIN miss and a call the length cap ended before the last question.
- The tier is read from what the turn did: the hand-offs it posted, the guard's hits, the refusal and the misses. A tool's name alone does not decide it, because one tool, `record_answer`, ends one call with a booking offer and another with a hand-off.
- The outbound caps and lines [default]. An outbound call that has run MAX_OUTBOUND_CALL_SECONDS (480 by default) ends between turns with the length line worded "the time limit", because five posted questions and a booking do not fit in the inbound 300. The outbound stop line: "Understood. This line won't call this number again. Goodbye." The kill switch closes an outbound call with the kill line, as it closes an inbound one, and stops the dialler.

## 8. Done means

A verified candidate hangs up inside three minutes with a slot confirmed out loud or a callback promised, having heard nothing about how they are doing. Every boundary case in `evals/cases` passes; the regression pass rate holds at or above `evals/baseline.json`, less one regression case: one flake does not turn the gate red, two do (DR-017). The median brain time per turn stays inside DR-001's budget on the happy paths, read from `make eval`. Every turn of every call leaves its spans in the trace store, readable in the cockpit. `make gate` is green.

For the outbound call (section 9): an applicant who applied with consent inside the calling window is called within five minutes of the application, hears that the line is automated before anything else, confirms by PIN, hears every posted question in the posted words with one fixed line after each answer, and hangs up either with an interview slot confirmed out loud (every posted requirement met) or with the promised recruiter call (not met), having heard nothing about how they are doing. The proposed outbound cases (CL-041 to CL-056, `prompts/08`) pass once James has approved them into `evals/`. The scorecard's four outbound rows (section 9.14) print with their method and n. No raw phone number appears in a trace, a log, a post, an email or any file but the contacts store (section 9.13).

## 9. The outbound call

Ruled by James on 5 November 2026 and written the same day by lane 0 of the outbound build. Record: `docs/decisions/DR-025-outbound-screening.md` (DRAFT); work item: `prompts/08-outbound-screening.md`. ACME's applicants for hourly roles apply on a careers form that is not part of this repository; the form posts a signed application, and the line calls the applicant back to ask the role's posted questions. A person makes every hiring decision.

1. **Intake.** `POST /applications` takes a JSON application signed with HMAC-SHA256 under INTAKE_SIGNING_KEY over a timestamp, a nonce and the body. A missing key, a bad signature, a missing nonce, a timestamp more than 300 seconds from now, a nonce seen in the last ten minutes, an unknown key or a value outside the schema is refused before anything is stored, so a captured request cannot be replayed inside the timestamp's window. While OUTBOUND_ENABLED is off, intake answers 503 and stores nothing, so no application waits to be called days late as if it had just arrived. The schema: first name, role id (one of `data/roles.yaml`), a US mobile number in E.164 form, an IANA time zone in the US, a four-digit PIN the applicant chose, and the consent record (the consent text's version, accepted true or false, the time, the source). The answer is 202 with the application code, `ACME-` and four digits, and never echoes the number or the PIN. An application without consent, from a number that opted out, or from a number not on the allow-list (9.3) is stored and shown in the cockpit and never dialled, and its raw number is not kept.
2. **The window.** A dial is placed only from 09:00 to 20:00, Monday to Saturday, in the applicant's own time zone [default], and never on a Sunday: a window setting that names Sunday stops the process at start, and no code path returns a Sunday time. An application that arrives outside the window waits for its next opening. The federal limit for a telephone solicitation is 08:00 to 21:00 at the called party's location (47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1)); the line keeps an hour inside it at each end. A CALL_WINDOW that starts before 08:00 or ends after 21:00 stops the process at start.
3. **The caps.** The dialler runs only when OUTBOUND_ENABLED is on, and it is off by default. Intake takes an application only then (9.1). It places at most MAX_OUTBOUND_PER_DAY dials (20 by default, 100 at most) on one Central-time day, counted when a dial is placed, and one outbound call at a time (MAX_CONCURRENT_OUTBOUND, 1). An outbound call ends at MAX_OUTBOUND_CALL_SECONDS (480 by default, 900 at most, section 7), and Twilio's own TimeLimit on the call is that plus 60 seconds, so a fault in the line still ends the call. No dial is placed while `var/kill` or `var/no-dial` exists, in the first 120 seconds after the task starts, or after a speech service answers AccessDenied (the budget stop, DR-023). A cap that reads as zero or as nonsense stops the process at start.
   While the line is a demo, the dialler may call only the numbers in OUTBOUND_ALLOWED_NUMBERS, a secret holding the probe's number and the operator's. Code checks the list immediately before every dial, and `/voice` checks it again; a number not on it is never dialled. An application whose number is not on the list is stored, shown in the cockpit as "not dialled: number not on the allow-list", and never dialled. This is the control against a keyholder or a typo dialling a stranger. Lifting the list needs its own decision record after counsel's read (`prompts/08`, open decision 2).
4. **Attempts.** At most MAX_DIAL_ATTEMPTS (3) dials per application: the first two minutes after intake or at the window's next opening, the second two hours after the first, the third on the next calling day at the middle of the window (14:30 in the applicant's zone for the default window) [default]. Busy, no answer, failed, a machine, or the kill switch ending the call schedules the next attempt; one the kill switch caused waits until the switch is off. A person reached, an opt-out, a wrong person or a second PIN miss ends the dialling for that application. After the third attempt the applicant can still call in (3.10).
5. **Voicemail.** Machine detection is Twilio's asynchronous detection that waits for the end of the greeting (`MachineDetection=DetectMessageEnd`, `AsyncAmd=true`), and no other mode. On the first attempt only, a machine hears the voicemail line once and the call ends; on a later attempt a machine ends the call with no message [default]. A voicemail never names the role.
6. **The disclosure.** The opening (Never 3) is said by Twilio's `<Say>` before `<Connect><Stream>`, so no barge-in can cut it and it covers the seconds the stream takes to start. Its first words name ACME and say the line is automated (47 CFR 64.1200(b)(1)). The stream's first line is the PIN ask. A word the applicant says over the opening is not heard, because the stream is not yet open; the PIN ask then says how to stop.
7. **Confirmation.** `confirm_applicant(pin)` against the PIN chosen at intake (3.6). Until it matches: a transcript made only of hello words ("hello", "hi", "hey", "yes", "yeah", "speaking", "this is she", "this is he", the applicant's first name or "this is" and that name, with fillers among them) is not a turn, it never barges in, and the line says the PIN ask once more by code, at most twice on a call. "Wrong number", "not me" or "you have the wrong person" goes to `stop_calls()`, so a number that changed hands is never called again.
8. **The questions.** After the PIN, the line says the intro line and then the role's posted questions, word for word from `data/roles.yaml`, in the posted order, by code. Each question states its posted requirement in its own words, the same for every applicant, so no line ever reacts to one answer. The model's one job per answer is to map the applicant's words to a posted value with `record_answer`; anything that is not plainly one of them is `unclear`. A skip is `declined` and counts as asked. The first `unclear` on a question gets the unclear line and the question once more; a second is recorded as `unclear`. A question the line has said and recorded is asked; the gate (9.10) counts nothing else. An applicant who calls in and verifies resumes at the first question not yet recorded.
9. **The fixed lines**, said by code, word for word [default]:
   - The opening (Twilio's `<Say>`): "Hello, this is ACME's automated hiring line, calling for {first name} about a job application. I'm Gus, an automated voice, and this call is transcribed so a recruiter can read your answers. No audio is kept."
   - The PIN ask (the stream's first line): "To go on, please say the four-digit PIN you chose when you applied. Or say stop, and this line won't call you again."
   - PIN_RETRY_LINE: "That PIN didn't match. Please say the four digits once more."
   - PIN_SECOND_MISS_LINE: "That didn't match either, so I can't go on with the questions on this call. I can ask a recruiter to call this number back. Would you like that?"
   - The intro: "Thank you. I have {count} questions the employer posted for the {role title} role. I'll note each answer as you give it, and you can skip any. After the last one, I'll either offer interview times or tell you when a recruiter will call."
   - After each answer, then the next question: "Noted: {value}." The value is said as the applicant's own choice: "yes", "no", "skipped" for declined, "not clear" for unclear, the option's posted words, or the number with its posted unit, the unit's first word in the singular for the number 1 ("1 year").
   - Unclear, then the same question once more: "Sorry, I didn't catch that."
   - Every requirement met: "That's every question, thank you. A recruiter will read all your answers before the interview. The nearest open interview times are {up to three slots}. Which suits you?"
   - Met with no open slot: "That's every question, thank you. There are no open interview times right now, so a recruiter will call you within one business day to set one."
   - A posted requirement not met: "That's every question, thank you. For this role, the posting asks for {what the posting asks for}. A recruiter will read your answers and call you within one business day." `{what the posting asks for}` is each such question's posted `asks` words, joined by "and"; nothing else from the answers is said.
   - The voicemail, first attempt only: "This is ACME's automated hiring line with a message for {first name} about a job application. Please call us back at {the line's number}, or we'll try again later. To stop these calls, call that number and say stop." The number is spoken from TWILIO_LINE_NUMBER at run time and is never written in a file.
   - The outbound stop line (section 7): "Understood. This line won't call this number again. Goodbye."
   - An applicant past the PIN who asks for a person ("person", "a person", "a real person", "recruiter", with please): "A recruiter will call you within one business day. Goodbye." [default; the words were proposed on 2026-11-05 and James may reword them]
   - None of these, and no reply on an outbound call, says qualify, qualified, meets, met the requirement, eligible, failed, rejected, passed, or any of their negatives (the guard's new rules, `prompts/08`).
10. **The gate.** One pure function, `candidate_line/tools/rules.py`, reads the role's posted questions and the applicant's recorded answers and returns whether every question was asked and whether every posted requirement is met. Met means every question has a recorded answer whose value meets its posted rule; `declined` and `unclear` meet no rule. Its result decides only which fixed line follows the last answer and whether `list_open_slots` and `book_slot` may run for that applicant. It has no score, weight, rank, threshold over several answers, or comparison between applicants, and `data/roles.yaml` may hold only the closed rule forms of DR-025 (equals yes or no; one of the posted options; at least, at most or between two posted numbers); the loader refuses any other key. The model never decides the gate.
11. **The hand-off.** A not-met screen, and a met screen with no open slot, post a tier-2 hand-off of the kind "screening hand-off" at once. A met screen whose call ends after the line offered times and nothing was booked posts the same hand-off when the call ends, with the promised time one business day after the call. Its email names the applicant by first name and code, the role, the time the line promised the call by, and the link to the applicant's record in the cockpit (`/applicants/{code}`). It never carries an answer, the applicant's words, which requirement was not met, or the number. The promised time is one business day (Monday to Friday) after the screen ended, in Central time [default].
12. **Opt-out.** On an outbound call, or from an applicant on an inbound call, "stop" (section 7's stop words) and "don't call me", "stop calling", "take me off", "wrong number" or "not me" end the call with the outbound stop line and cancel every dial still due, at once (the wrong-person phrases "wrong number", "not me" and "you have the wrong person" only before the PIN matches, as 9.7 says; after it, "not me" is an answer), though the rule allows ten business days (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10)). On an inbound call, a caller who has not verified counts as the applicant for this when the number they call from is one an application gave, matched by its HMAC and never kept, because the voicemail line tells them to call and say stop. The opt-out keeps an HMAC of the number, so a later application from the same number is stored and never dialled. After the PIN, an ask for a person that is the whole utterance requests a recruiter's callback by code, with no model call, and ends the call with the recruiter line (9.9); inside an answer or a question, the word goes to the model.
13. **Retention and access.** The raw phone number lives only in the contacts store (`CONTACTS_PATH`, on the encrypted volume, mode 600), read only by the dialler. Every trace, log, post, email and cockpit page carries the masked form. A raw number is deleted on the first dialler tick after CONTACTS_RETENTION_DAYS (30 by default) from intake, at once on an opt-out, and at once on a deletion request. Answers, dials and applications are deleted on the first tick after ANSWERS_RETENTION_DAYS (1460 by default, four years, the California regulations' minimum for automated-decision data) [default]. The sweep runs on every tick whether dialling is on or off. A deletion request, or the end of the answers' retention, removes the application, its answers, its dials, its contact and the trace-store record of every call they name (the call, its spans and its hand-offs), and keeps only the consent record's version and time and the number's HMAC, so the opt-out still holds. INTAKE_SIGNING_KEY and OUTBOUND_ALLOWED_NUMBERS live in Secrets Manager and reach the task as secret references, never as plain environment values.
14. **The scorecard's outbound rows** (`make scorecard`), each with its method and n: minutes from application to first contact (to the first dial a person answered; median, worst, n; in-window arrivals apart from those that waited; never reached counted); answers captured per applicant (recorded answers other than `unclear` over the role's question count; median, n; declined beside it); recruiter minutes saved (an estimate on the synthetic baseline `data/baseline_screens.csv`); and the promise as a check row (met screens offered slots, n of n; not-met hand-offs called back inside the promised time, n of n; short of n is a defect).
